---
id: FCT-0012
title: Empirical subtitle-timing measurements show a 97% completion threshold protects live-action movie endings better than 94%
status: active
evidence: "Measured single-part theatrical-frame-rate English subtitle endings against catalog runtimes across 14 films (81m to 181m): >= 94% fired prematurely before final non-stinger dialogue/ending audio on 10 of 14 films (71.4%, including The Dark Knight at 94.99%, Pulp Fiction at 96.89%, Interstellar at 96.90%, and Oppenheimer at 96.28%), whereas >= 97% avoided premature clearing on all 11 live-action films (100%) and 12 of 14 films overall (85.7%)."
---

## Statement
End credits do not consume a constant `6%` fraction across feature films of varying lengths. While multi-studio blockbuster effects epics can carry `9–13 minutes` of closing credits (`91%–93.5%`), short-to-medium films and drama epics commonly finish their final scene `3–7.5 minutes` before the end of the file (`94.3%–97.0%`). Raising the automatic movie completion cutoff from `94%` to `97%` prevents premature removal during climaxes and final monologues across measured live-action films (`100m–181m`), leaving only animated features whose closing songs or mid-credits dialogue extend to `99.6%–100%`.
